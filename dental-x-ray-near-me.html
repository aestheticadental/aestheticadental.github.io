How Safe are Digital Dental X-Rays? (Radiation Facts)

Many patients worry about X-ray radiation. Here is how modern RVG sensors compare to natural daily environmental exposure:

~0.005 mSv

1 Digital RVG Dental X-Ray

Micro-dose. Equivalent to natural environmental radiation absorbed during a 1-hour commercial airplane flight.

~0.010 mSv

1 Day of Normal Life

Natural background radiation from sunshine, soil, and rocks in Maharashtra equals twice the dose of a digital dental X-ray.

~0.040 mSv

Old Conventional Dental Film

Traditional non-digital film units required 4x to 8x higher radiation exposure than modern digital sensors.

~0.100 mSv

Standard Chest X-Ray

A single hospital chest radiograph equals approximately 20 individual digital dental RVG exposures.

Clinical Diagnoses

What Can a Digital Dental X-Ray Detect?

More than 50% of your tooth structure sits hidden under your gums and jawbone. RVG imaging is critical for:

🦷

Hidden Interdental Cavities

Tooth decay frequently begins in the tight contact points between two molars where toothbrushes cannot reach. An X-ray detects early enamel demineralization before severe pain develops.

⚡

Deep Nerve & Pulp Infections

When bacteria penetrate the inner pulp chamber, pus and periapical abscesses collect at the root tip. Digital RVG reveals the exact depth of infection for single-sitting root canals.

🦴

Bone Loss from Gum Disease

Advanced periodontitis erodes the alveolar bone that anchors your teeth into the jaw. Digital X-rays measure bone height to plan targeted deep scaling and periodontal splinting.

📐

Impacted Wisdom Teeth

Third molars often grow horizontally or tilted, pushing against adjacent second molars and causing nerve impingement. An X-ray shows the root curvature before safe extraction.

💥

Hidden Tooth & Root Fractures

Chewing hard food or sports injuries can cause hairline cracks through the root that cannot be seen with the naked eye, leading to chronic biting tenderness.

🧸

Children’s Developing Adult Teeth

Verifying whether adult permanent teeth are positioned correctly beneath milk teeth, checking for congenital missing teeth, or evaluating early childhood bottle caries.
