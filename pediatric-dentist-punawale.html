When Should Your Child First Visit a Pediatric Dentist?

Children should have their first dental checkup by their first birthday or within 6 months of their first milk tooth erupting. Early visits allow the dentist to examine healthy jaw development, detect early childhood caries (bottle rot), apply preventive fluoride varnishes, and guide parents on effective brushing habits—preventing childhood dental anxiety before problems begin.

Parent FAQs

Frequently Asked Questions About Children's Dentistry

Clear, direct answers for parents regarding first checkups, cavity prevention, and soothing anxious kids.

At what age should my child first visit a dentist? +

Pediatric dental associations recommend scheduling a child's first dental examination when their first tooth erupts, or by their first birthday. Early visits allow Dr. Prachi Gupta Garg to evaluate jaw growth, check for early childhood caries (bottle rot), and guide parents on proper infant oral hygiene.

Why are milk teeth important if they are going to fall out anyway? +

Primary (milk) teeth are vital for proper chewing, speech development, and facial muscle formation. Crucially, they act as natural space maintainers for incoming adult teeth. If a baby tooth is lost prematurely due to decay, neighboring teeth can shift, causing severe crowding and impaction of permanent teeth.

How does Dr. Prachi help anxious children feel comfortable? +

Dr. Prachi uses a gentle 'Tell-Show-Do' technique, explaining instruments in kid-friendly, non-threatening terms before beginning. We maintain a warm, welcoming environment with positive reinforcement and zero rushing, ensuring your child develops a lifelong positive association with dental care.

What are pit and fissure sealants, and does my child need them? +

Dental sealants are thin, protective resin coatings applied to the deep chewing grooves (fissures) of permanent back molars. They create a smooth barrier that blocks food particles and cavity-causing bacteria, reducing tooth decay risk by up to 80%.
