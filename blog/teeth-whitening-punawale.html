Why Do Teeth Turn Yellow or Dark? (It's Not Just About Brushing)

This is the part most "teeth whitening" content skips — and it's the most important part. There are two fundamentally different types of tooth discolouration, and they require completely different solutions.

1. Extrinsic Staining — The Kind Whitening Fixes

Extrinsic stains sit on or just below the outer surface of the enamel. They build up over time from:

  • Tea and coffee — the most common culprit in Punawale and across Pune, where chai is practically a food group
  • Tobacco — smoking and smokeless tobacco both cause significant yellow-brown staining
  • Red wine, dark juices, and cola
  • Certain foods — turmeric, tomato-based sauces, berries
  • Poor scaling habits — plaque and tartar accumulation darkens teeth over time

The good news: professional whitening is highly effective on extrinsic staining. Most patients see a clear difference after a single session.

2. Intrinsic Discolouration — The Kind Whitening Cannot Fix

Intrinsic discolouration comes from within the tooth structure itself. Common causes include:

  • Tetracycline antibiotic use during childhood (causes grey-brown banding)
  • Dental fluorosis (from excess fluoride during tooth development — shows as white or brown spots)
  • Trauma to a tooth causing internal bleeding (greying of a single tooth)
  • Natural ageing — as enamel thins, the darker dentine underneath shows through more

For intrinsic discolouration, whitening agents have limited effect. In these cases, composite bonding or porcelain veneers give far better aesthetic results — and that's exactly what I tell patients honestly during their consultation, rather than taking their money for whitening that won't meet their expectations.

How Professional Whitening Works at Aesthetica

Here's what a whitening appointment at our clinic in Punawale actually looks like, step by step.

Step 1 — Pre-Whitening Assessment (10–15 minutes)

Before anything touches your teeth, I examine them thoroughly. I check for cavities, gum health, existing restorations, and the type of staining present. If there's an active cavity or gum inflammation, we treat that first — whitening on unhealthy teeth is not something I recommend. I also take a shade reading so we have a baseline to compare against.

Step 2 — Scaling and Polishing (if required)

Surface tartar and plaque absorb whitening agents unevenly, leading to patchy results. If your teeth haven't been scaled recently, a polish before whitening gives much more uniform results. Many patients choose to combine their routine scaling appointment with whitening on the same visit.

Step 3 — Gum Protection

A protective barrier is applied to your gums before the whitening agent goes anywhere near your teeth. This is the step home kits skip — and it's a significant reason why professional whitening causes far less sensitivity than strips applied carelessly.

Step 4 — Application and Activation

The whitening gel is applied to the tooth surfaces and left for the prescribed time. Depending on the system used and the degree of staining, this active period typically runs 30–45 minutes. You're comfortably seated, and most patients spend this time on their phones or simply relaxing.

Step 5 — Shade Assessment and Aftercare Instructions

After rinsing, I compare the result against your baseline shade. The change is usually immediately visible and often quite dramatic — patients are frequently surprised in a good way. You'll receive specific aftercare instructions: what to eat and avoid for the next 48 hours, and how to maintain results.

Who Is a Good Candidate for Teeth Whitening?

Teeth whitening gives the best results for people who:

  • Have yellow or brown staining from tea, coffee, tobacco, or food
  • Have generally healthy teeth and gums (no active cavities or advanced gum disease)
  • Have not had extensive front-tooth restorations that would create a colour mismatch after whitening
  • Are 18 years or older (whitening is not recommended for children or adolescents)
  • Are not pregnant or breastfeeding

If you have crowns, veneers, or large fillings on your front teeth, whitening will lighten the surrounding natural teeth without changing the restoration colour — potentially making the mismatch more obvious. This is something we discuss honestly at your consultation, and there are solutions (like coordinating restoration replacement with whitening) that we can plan for you.

How Long Do Whitening Results Last?

This is the question almost every patient asks — and the honest answer is: it depends on your habits. Professional whitening results typically last 12–24 months. The main factors that shorten the duration:

  • High tea or coffee consumption (common in Pune — I won't pretend otherwise)
  • Tobacco use
  • Skipping routine dental cleanings

The main factors that extend results:

  • Rinsing with water immediately after consuming staining drinks
  • Using a whitening toothpaste (maintenance, not primary treatment)
  • Getting a scale and polish every 6 months
  • Doing a brief top-up whitening session once a year

Many of our Punawale patients come in for a whitening session before a wedding — their own or a family member's. If that's you, I'd recommend booking 2–3 weeks in advance rather than the day before, so there's no residual sensitivity on an important day and the colour has fully stabilised.

Teeth Whitening Sensitivity — What's Normal and What's Not

Mild tooth sensitivity for 24–48 hours after whitening is common and completely normal. It typically feels like a brief sharp sensation when consuming cold liquids. This resolves on its own.

What's not normal: persistent sensitivity beyond 72 hours, pain at rest, or gum soreness that doesn't improve. If any of these occur — which is rare with properly supervised professional whitening — contact us immediately.

To minimise sensitivity, I recommend:

  • Using a sensitivity toothpaste (like Sensodyne) for a week before your whitening appointment
  • Avoiding very hot or cold food and drinks for 24–48 hours post-treatment
  • Taking a mild painkiller before the appointment if you already know you have sensitive teeth

What Whitening Won't Do — Setting Realistic Expectations

I want to be direct about this, because I think some clinics oversell whitening. Here is what professional whitening will not do:

  • Turn deeply discoloured teeth (from tetracycline or fluorosis) to a bright white — these require veneers
  • Make your teeth the colour of paper or porcelain — natural-looking white is the realistic target
  • Work on crowns, bridges, composite fillings, or veneers — these materials do not respond to whitening agents
  • Permanently "fix" your teeth — it's a cosmetic treatment that needs maintenance

The patients I see who are happiest after whitening are the ones who came in with realistic expectations — and those expectations are shaped by an honest conversation at the consultation. That's what we do at Aesthetica.

Teeth Whitening FAQ — Punawale & Pune

How much does teeth whitening cost in Punawale?

Is professional whitening safe for sensitive teeth?

Yes, with precautions. If you have sensitive teeth, we recommend using a desensitising toothpaste for a week before your appointment and we adjust the whitening protocol accordingly. Mild post-treatment sensitivity for 24–48 hours is normal and resolves on its own. Prolonged sensitivity with professional treatment is rare.

How many sessions does teeth whitening take in Pune?

Most Aesthetica patients achieve their desired shade in a single 45–60 minute session. Patients with heavier staining from years of tea, coffee, or tobacco may benefit from a second session after 4–6 weeks. Results last 1–2 years with proper care and routine scaling.

Will whitening work if I drink a lot of chai or coffee?

Yes — tea and coffee staining is exactly the type of discolouration that responds best to professional whitening. Your results will last longer if you rinse with water after tea or coffee, and come in for a routine scale and polish every 6 months. A brief annual whitening top-up also helps maintain results.

Can I whiten my teeth if I have crowns or veneers?

Whitening agents do not affect the colour of crowns, veneers, or composite fillings. If you have visible restorations on front teeth, we assess whether whitening will create a colour mismatch and advise the best approach during your consultation. This is always discussed honestly before any treatment begins.
