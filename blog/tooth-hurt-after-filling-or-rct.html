Why Does a Tooth Hurt After a Filling or Root Canal?

By Dr. Prachi Gupta Garg (BDS) • Post-Treatment Care • Punawale, Pune

You just had a cavity filled or completed a root canal treatment, but a day later, the tooth feels tender when you bite down. It is natural to feel worried, but mild soreness is often a normal part of the body's healing response. Here is how to distinguish normal recovery from an issue that requires a quick visit.

1. Why Fillings Hurt: The "High Bite" Phenomenon

By far the most frequent cause of post-filling discomfort is a microscopic high spot on the new filling. Because your mouth was numb during the appointment, your bite may have felt fine chairside. However, even a 50-micron elevation means that tooth takes the entire brunt of your jaw force every time you chew.

The Fix: A simple 2-minute bite adjustment with articulating paper. Dr. Prachi gently polishes down the high contact point, and the tenderness subsides within 24 to 48 hours.

2. Why Teeth Hurt After Root Canal Treatment (RCT)

Patients often assume: "If the nerve is gone, how can the tooth still hurt?" While the internal pulp nerve inside the tooth has been removed, the periodontal ligament holding the root in the jawbone is very much alive. During treatment, cleaning files reach the root apex, creating minor, temporary inflammation in this surrounding ligament (periapical periodontitis).

This localized tenderness typically peaks at 48 hours and fades steadily over 5 to 7 days. Mild anti-inflammatory pain medication easily manages this period. Read our complete root canal aftercare instructions.

3. Deep Pulpitis After Large Cavity Fillings

If tooth decay was deep and approached the pulp chamber, the tooth nerve can experience reversible hyperemia (inflammation) after being cleaned. You may feel a quick 2-second sensitivity to ice water that dissipates immediately. If the cold sensitivity lingers for minutes or turns into throbbing night pain, the pulp may be irreversible, indicating the need for root canal therapy.

When Is Tooth Pain a Warning Sign?

Contact Our Clinic If You Notice:

  • Visible swelling in the gum or cheek near the treated tooth.
  • Sharp pain that awakens you in the middle of the night.
  • Fever or a foul, salty taste in your mouth.
  • Tenderness that worsens after day 5 rather than improving.
