Dental Crown Types & Costs After Root Canal: PFM vs Zirconia vs E-Max

Completing a root canal treatment removes pain and halts dental infection, but the job is only half-done until the tooth is protected with a custom dental crown (cap). Here is how to select the best crown material for your smile and budget.

Why Is a Dental Crown Essential After a Root Canal?

During single-sitting root canal treatment, the infected blood supply and dental pulp are thoroughly removed, shaped, and sealed. While this saves the physical tooth from extraction, the lack of an internal blood supply causes the remaining enamel and dentin to lose moisture.

Under the heavy chewing forces of your jaws (up to 200 pounds of pressure on molar teeth), an unprotected, dehydrated tooth can easily crack vertically down to the bone, forcing an unavoidable extraction. A custom dental crown acts as a 360-degree protective helmet, absorbing masticatory shock and restoring full bite strength.

Why Dr. Prachi Recommends Metal-Free Zirconia for Molars

At Aesthetica Dental Clinic, monolithic Zirconia crowns have largely replaced older PFM caps for posterior teeth. With precision CAD/CAM digital 3D milling:

  • No Gray Lines: Unlike metal crowns, Zirconia never causes dark discoloration at the gum margin.
  • Enamel-Friendly: Highly polished zirconia does not abrade or wear away the opposing natural teeth when chewing.
  • Biocompatible & Hypoallergenic: Completely metal-free, eliminating allergic reactions or metallic aftertastes.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a dental crown last?

With good oral hygiene (twice-daily brushing, flossing, and 6-month cleanings), high-grade Zirconia and E-max crowns typically last 15 to 20+ years, and often a lifetime.

Can a crowned tooth get a cavity again?

The porcelain crown itself cannot decay. However, bacteria can gather at the margin where the crown meets your natural tooth root if plaque is not cleaned away. Maintaining proper flossing prevents margin decay.
